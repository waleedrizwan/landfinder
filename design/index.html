Three directions for Cheapest Land

Each one uses 42 real listings from today’s data. Filters work on that sample; links go to the real listings.

A. Price list

A ranked table instead of a grid of cards. You compare prices by reading down one column, and the filters sit in a sidebar with how many listings each choice has.

  • Best for scanning: more listings fit on screen than with cards.
  • Counts beside each country and type show where the cheap land is before you click.
  • On a phone the rows fold into price on the left, details below.

B. Drawn to scale

Every listing shows its land as a square, drawn to the same scale as a football pitch. “0.17 acres” means little to most people; a patch a tenth the size of a pitch does.

  • The one memorable thing: you see straight away that $582 buys more land than $552.
  • Listings sit in a grid of parcels split by survey lines, coloured by type of land.
  • Type of property becomes one row of buttons instead of a dropdown.

C. Atlas

The list and the map on one screen, and the filters read as a sentence: “Any land under $2,000 in Europe”. Hover a row and its price lights up on the map.

  • No switching tabs to see where a listing is.
  • The three filters people use most are always in view; the rest go under More filters.
  • On a phone the map sits above the list.

In all three

  • Titles written in capitals are shown in sentence case.
  • The price in the listing’s own currency sits under the converted one, in place of “Converted from…”.
  • A real typeface and tabular figures, so prices line up.
  • Light and dark themes, and a layout that works on a phone.
